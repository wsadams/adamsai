Phase 0 — Reference

Foundation — context and why each step matters

All tasks are in the Schedule tab under Phase 0. This tab explains what to choose and why.

Separate Solitude Lighting accounts from D2Jay — completely

A homeowner looking for kitchen lighting has nothing in common with an artist booking a music video. Different audience, different tone, different content, different trust signals. You already have @solitudelighting on Instagram. Create a separate TikTok, Threads, and Facebook Business Page for Solitude Lighting only. Never cross-post between D2Jay and Solitude Lighting accounts. The D2Jay personal page can bridge early — repost one Solitude piece per week from your personal account to warm up existing followers while the new accounts build. After 30 posts, stop relying on D2Jay as a bridge.

Bank account options

Mercury (mercury.com — online, no fees, best for startups), Relay (relayfi.com — clean and simple for solo contractors), or walk into a local Citi or Chase branch. All three are solid. The most important thing is that you open one and use it — every dollar from Solitude Lighting goes in here, never mixed with personal money.

What to delay until after client 1

Business insurance ($30–$50/month), virtual mailbox ($10–$20/month), and professional email through Google Workspace ($6/month) are all worth doing — but they add monthly cost before you have proven income. Wait until your first paid install. Do not add expenses to prove a hypothesis.

The zero-followers question

Starting with zero followers is real — but it matters less than you think on Reels and TikTok, which push new content to non-followers based on watch time and engagement. The real answer is this: do not run paid ads on new accounts until you have at least 9 posts and some proof of work showing. Instead, lean hard on Facebook groups and Nextdoor first — those channels do not require followers. The algorithm treats a compelling Reel from a brand-new account the same as one from a million-follower account if people watch it. Post good content. Reply to everything. The followers come from the content, not the other way around.

Speaking to the homeowner's problem — not your service

The homeowner's problem is not "I need LED lights." Their problem is "My home doesn't feel like me. Every time I walk into my kitchen I feel something is off and I don't know why. My guests notice. Every piece of proof, every caption, every post needs to speak to that feeling — not the product. Show them the after, help them feel what it is like to be in that space, and let them imagine it in their home.

All Phase 3 tasks are in the Schedule tab. This tab has scripts, the math, and the strategic context.

The math that gets you to 3 clients

50 LinkedIn connection requests → 15% reply rate = 7 to 8 conversations → 30% close rate = 2 to 3 booked installs. That is your first month of outreach producing your first clients. One conversation with one realtor who closes 30 homes a year could produce 5 to 15 referrals over the following 24 months. You are not selling to individuals. You are building pipelines that keep producing.

LinkedIn follow-up message — after they connect

"Hey [Name], appreciate the connection. Short version: I install LED kitchen and smart home lighting for new homeowners in PG County and Charles County. A lot of my ideal clients just bought in Waldorf or Bowie and want to upgrade from builder grade. If any of your buyers ever ask about that kind of upgrade, I'd love to be your go-to referral. I pay $150 per booking, no strings. Worth a quick chat?"

Quote follow-up sequence — Day 3, 7, 14

Day 3: "Hey, just checking in to see if you had any questions about the quote." Day 7: "I have a slot open the weekend of [date] — wanted to offer it to you first before I book it." Day 14: "Last reach out — if timing isn't right yet, no worries at all. Happy to connect when you're ready." Social vs Google — two different jobs
