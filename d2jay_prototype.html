FREQUENTLY
ASKED

Everything you need to know before reaching out. Still have questions?

Ask Anything
How much does a music video cost?
Music video packages start at $750 for a performance-based single-location shoot, $1,500 for a standard story-concept production, and $2,500–$3,500 for a cinematic multi-location production. Drone add-ons, extra shoot days, and VFX are available. Let's talk about your vision and build the right package.
Do you have a studio or physical location?
No — and that's intentional. I bring professional cinema-grade equipment and full creative direction directly to your location. Whether that's a rooftop in DC, a venue in Maryland, or a destination out of state, the production comes to you. This means no studio booking fees passed to you and locations that actually match your vision.
What does wedding video/photo coverage look like?
Wedding packages include photo only ($2,200+), video only ($2,400+), or a combined photo + film package starting at $3,800. Coverage includes getting-ready, ceremony, reception, and after-party. All packages include a full digital gallery, a cinematic highlight film, and all commercial usage rights. Engagement sessions and drone footage are available as add-ons.
Do you work outside the DMV area?
Yes. I'm based in the DC, Maryland, and Virginia area and serve the greater DMV market locally. I'm also available for travel bookings nationwide — including Atlanta, where I've worked extensively in the independent music scene. Travel rates apply for locations outside the DMV.
How far out do you book?
Wedding dates typically book 6–12 months in advance. Music video and brand shoots can often be scheduled within 2–4 weeks depending on availability. A deposit secures your date. Reach out as early as possible for peak season (May–October for weddings).
What do I need to bring to a shoot?
Just yourself and your vision. I bring all camera and audio equipment, lighting, stabilizers, and drone gear. For music videos, I'll work with you on a creative treatment beforehand so we arrive on set with a clear shot list. The less you have to think about the gear, the more you can focus on the performance.
